The **Civil Aviation Authority of Thailand (CAAT)** has announced that **from 16 October 2026**, airport security staff may open and inspect checked baggage **without the passenger present** when X-ray screening gives reasonable grounds to suspect a prohibited item. The aim is to avoid delaying flights while staff look for the bag's owner.

## What exactly changes

- **Not random.** CAAT says bags will not be opened routinely, only when screening raises suspicion.
- **Designated area, on video.** Inspections take place in a designated area and are recorded, and the records are kept for review.
- **A notice in your bag.** A *Notice of Baggage Inspection* is left inside to tell you the bag was opened.
- **Prohibited items are confiscated** and not returned.

## Which airports

Airports of Thailand (AOT) published the guideline for its six airports: **Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang in Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Chiang Rai (Mae Fah Luang), Phuket and Hat Yai**. Samui Airport has also published the CAAT notice. For other airports, check with the airport or your airline.

## Locks: use a TSA lock

CAAT recommends a **TSA-approved lock**, which security can open with a master key and relock without damage. **Other locks, straps and zips may be cut**, and according to AOT passengers cannot claim compensation for them. The alternative is to leave the bag unlocked with nothing valuable inside.

## Do not pack these in checked baggage

- **Power banks and spare lithium batteries**: cabin bag only.
- **Flammable sprays and chemicals.**
- **Explosives and firearms.**

## Keep these in your cabin bag

Passport and important documents, gold, jewellery and cash, laptop and camera, and **medicines with your prescription**, especially if you are travelling for treatment.

## Complaints

Contact your airline, file a complaint at [complaint.caat.or.th](https://complaint.caat.or.th), or call the AOT call centre on **1722**.
