Bangkok's international hospitals receive patients from all over the Gulf every year — for full check-ups, second opinions, surgery and long treatment plans. A little preparation makes the difference between a stressful trip and a smooth one.

## Which visa do you need?

Since 15 September 2026, Gulf citizens can enter Thailand **visa-free for 30 days, for tourism**. That may be enough for a short check-up combined with a holiday — but if **treatment is the main purpose** of your trip, or your stay could run past 30 days, a medical visa is the safer choice:

| Visa | Typical use | Length of stay |
|---|---|---|
| Tourist visa for medical treatment (TR / MT) | Treatment or check-ups up to about two months | Less than 60 days |
| Non-Immigrant O for medical treatment | Longer treatment, recovery, or a companion staying with the patient | More than 60 days |

Both are applied for online through the official portal **[thaievisa.go.th](https://www.thaievisa.go.th)**. Thai embassies advise applying **at least 15 working days before you travel**. Requirements differ by embassy, but you should expect to need a letter or appointment confirmation from the Thai hospital, your passport, and proof that you can cover your costs. Companions (for example a spouse or parent) may need their own application — check with the embassy that covers your country.

## Before you travel

- **Send your medical reports in advance.** Lab results, scans and previous diagnoses help the hospital choose the right specialist and give you a cost estimate.
- **List your medications** with their doses, and bring them in their original packaging.
- **Ask about fasting.** Most full check-ups include blood tests that require fasting — the hospital will tell you exactly how many hours.
- **Plan your days.** A full check-up usually takes one day; results and the doctor's explanation may be the same day or the next. Leave time for follow-up appointments.
- **Check your insurance** and bring a card or cash for deposits.

## On the day

Arrive early, bring your passport, and wear comfortable clothes. Having someone who speaks Arabic and Thai with you makes it much easier to ask questions and understand the results.
